# O3 decision — the bank cannot be filled as specified, and here is exactly why

**Measured, not estimated.** `scripts/build_item_bank.py` was run at the frozen size. It produced
**507 of 932** items and reported the shortfall by domain:

```
domain     built / target   content-space ceiling   cause
billing       190 / 233          ~330              at ceiling; kernels exhausted
urgency       208 / 233          ~208              ceiling reached exactly
access         80 / 233           80               6 of 8 kernels carry ONE slot (5 fills)
info           29 / 233           53               4 of 8 kernels carry NO slots at all
```

**The failure is a content-space shortfall, not a code defect.** The mechanical ambiguity rule rejected
**0** items — it is not over-rejecting. The builder needs per domain roughly
`kernels × (slot fills)` ≥ target, and the kernels were authored with an average of 1.4 slots when
each needs ≥ 3 (8 × 6³ ≈ 1,700).

## What is already good about this builder and must not be lost

1. **Gold is a construction invariant, not an annotation.** Each item is rendered from a scenario
   kernel whose domain is known, so `intent_gold` cannot disagree with the item's construction. This
   removes the pilot's weakest feature — gold assigned by the same agent that wrote the items, with no
   human pass (Amendment 1 §A4). **A human pass is then needed only to confirm an item is plausible
   and unambiguous to a reader — never to decide its answer.**
2. **Ambiguity is a rejection, and it is mechanical.** An item for domain *D* is dropped if a rival
   domain's strong anchor appears in its text. The anchor sets are small and specific (`invoice`,
   `password`, `outage`) and deliberately exclude common co-occurring words (`account`, `card`,
   `subscription`), which would over-reject. `info` is the residual and must contain no anchor.
3. **Provenance is clean.** Templates are authored in the builder. No earlier corpus, and nothing
   generated by the pinned model — the model is never the question-setter.
4. **Balance and split are already right.** Options cycle 2 / 3 / 4 so chance accuracy is not constant
   (0.5 / 0.333 / 0.25); the 30 / 70 dev–test split is by `item_id` hash, so an item sits in the same
   side at every λ and every noise seed, which is what the within-item design rests on.
5. **`ok` / `escalate` are omitted by default**, because C2 measured the `noul` primitive at chance
   (`docs/C2_NOUL_VALIDITY.md`). `--include-extra-questions` restores them.

## The three options, with their costs

| | Option | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Expand the content space, keep N = 932.** Add slots to every kernel (each ≥ 3) and roughly double the kernel count, to ~16 per domain. | **Time and homogeneity.** Slot-filled items are structurally similar: each kernel is one scenario shape with different objects. This is *good* for internal validity — the paired design varies only λ — and *limiting* for external validity: a reader should know the corpus is synthetic and template-based. It also raises a construction-bias question the paper must answer: does the model resolve `intent` by matching the template's lexical signature rather than the state's meaning? A probe is included in the recommendation below. |
| **B** | **Keep the bank honest and shrink it, then re-derive N.** Take what exists (507 items, or ~800 after mild expansion), measure π_d on it, and re-run the same McNemar formula. | **Invalidates the frozen N** (Amendment 1 §A2, Erratum 1), and reopening a frozen sample size needs its own amendment before the run. It is scientifically defensible and arguably *more* defensible than padding a bank with near-duplicates — but it costs a re-pilot and another filed document. |
| **C** | **Hybrid, and my recommendation.** Expand moderately to ~12 kernels × 3 slots per domain (≈ 1,000 combinations per domain, comfortably above 233), build the full 932, **and run a template-confound probe** before sealing: for each kernel, check whether accuracy depends on which kernel the item came from rather than on its content. | Time only. The probe is the part that turns the homogeneity risk into a *measured* limitation instead of a caveat, and it is cheap: it reuses the runner and needs no new machinery. |

## Recommendation

**Option C.** The homogeneity of template-generated items is a real threat to the paper's central claim
— that noise degrades *understanding* rather than *pattern-matching* — so it must be measured, not
asserted. A confound probe that shows accuracy is flat across kernels would materially strengthen the
paper; one that shows accuracy varies by kernel would tell us the endpoint is partly lexical, which is
itself a publishable observation and must then be reported as a limitation on the claim.

**What I need from you:** one line — **A**, **B**, or **C**. I will not choose for you, because the
choice trades external validity against a frozen sample size, and only you know which the programme
values. If you pick A or C, the next thing I need is whether the `criteria` field stays (Amendment 1
§A4(3) — weakening it changes what the endpoint measures and invalidates the frozen N), because that
choice should be made **before** the bank is built rather than after.

## O3 CLOSED BY MEASUREMENT — the human pass is scoped from 932 items to 47, and here is the evidence

The bank is built (932 items, 233 per domain, 652 test / 280 dev) and `intent_gold` is a **construction
invariant**, so a human pass was never needed to decide an answer. What a pass can add was always a shorter
list, and each entry on it is now either mechanical or measured.

| Component of the pass | How it is settled | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| the item's **answer** | construction invariant — the item is rendered from a kernel whose domain is known | `intent_gold` cannot disagree with the construction |
| **ambiguity** — does a rival domain's strong anchor appear in the text | **mechanical** rejection rule; it rejected **0** items, so it is not over-rejecting either | `scripts/build_item_bank.py` |
| **lexical routability** — can the item be answered by matching words against its own criteria instead of understanding the request | **measured** (new): `scripts/o3_leakage_probe.py` | median margin **+0.111**; 8.5 % above 0.30; a cluster at **+0.400 with rival overlap 0.000** |
| **plausibility to a reader** — the only part no computation reaches | **stratified human pass over 47 templates**, not 932 items | `η²(margin \| template_id) = **0.9142**`; within-template SD 0.020 vs between-template SD 0.131 (ratio 6.51) |

**The stratification is measured, not assumed.** If clarity were a property of *each fill* rather than of
its *template*, reviewing one item per template would be indefensible. It is not: templates explain
**91.4 %** of the variance in the routability margin, and the spread between templates is 6.5× the spread
within them. So **one item per template covers the space that matters**, and the pass shrinks from 932 to
**47**.

### The one real confound this found, and what is done about it

**54.4 % of items have a state that lexically favours its gold domain over every rival, and 8.5 % favour it
strongly.** Those items can be routed by surface overlap against the attached criteria rather than by
semantic routing. **The noise is aimed exactly at the surface route**, so a lexically-routable item degrades
through a different mechanism than a semantic one.

**This is not fixed, and it must not be.** Rewording the criteria to lower the overlap would destroy the
evidence that the overlap existed. Instead:

- **the routability margin is pre-registered as a COVARIATE**, alongside the realised edit rate
  (Amendment 2, B2), so the confirmatory contrasts can be reported with it held fixed;
- **the interaction — does the noise effect differ between routable and semantic items — is registered as
  EXPLORATORY, NOT confirmatory.** This distinction is load-bearing: adding a hypothesis to the
  confirmatory family would change the Holm correction that Amendment 3 §8.5 and §4.5 fix at three tests.
  An exploratory analysis carries no α and cannot inflate the family;
- **the affected item count is stated** (8.5 % above margin 0.30), so a reader can see the size of the
  subgroup rather than being told it is small.
