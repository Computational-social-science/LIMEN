# Appendix D — Analysis, Power and Results

## 1 Estimators

The design is paired at the **unit** level: each (item, seed) yields one clean and one noisy observation. The exact paired permutation test is defined as follows. For a given contrast $T(\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{Y})$ computed on the paired vectors $\mathbf{X}$ (clean) and $\mathbf{Y}$ (noisy), the permutation distribution is generated by independently swapping the two observations within each unit:

$$
T^*_k = T\bigl(\mathbf{X}^{(\pi_k)}, \mathbf{Y}^{(\pi_k)}\bigr), \quad k = 1,\dots, 2^U
$$

where $U = 1956$ is the number of exchangeable units (652 items $\times$ 3 seeds), and $\pi_k$ ranges over all $2^U$ independent sign-flip vectors. The two-sided $p$-value is

$$
p = \frac{1}{2^U} \sum_{k=1}^{2^U} \mathbb{1}\bigl\{ |T^*_k| \ge |T_{\text{obs}}| \bigr\}.
\tag{1}
$$

This test is **exact** under the exchangeability assumption: for every unit $u$, the joint distribution of $(X_u, Y_u)$ is invariant under swapping. In symbols, $(X_u, Y_u) \stackrel{d}{=} (Y_u, X_u)$ for all $u$, and units are mutually independent. No distributional assumption and no asymptotics are required; the test is the limiting case of a random-intercept model carrying only the item effect.

The within-arm AUC estimand for hypothesis H1.2′ is the probability that a randomly drawn correct trial receives a higher confidence than a randomly drawn error trial **within the same arm** (clean or noisy). Formally, for arm $a \in \{\text{clean}, \text{noisy}\}$,

$$
\text{AUC}_a = \Pr\bigl( c_i > c_j \mid y_i = 1, y_j = 0, \text{arm}=a \bigr) + \tfrac{1}{2}\Pr\bigl( c_i = c_j \mid y_i = 1, y_j = 0, \text{arm}=a \bigr),
\tag{2}
$$

where $c$ is the model's reported confidence and $y \in \{0,1\}$ indicates correctness. The contrast tested is $\Delta = \text{AUC}_{\text{noisy}} - \text{AUC}_{\text{clean}}$, and its exact permutation distribution is obtained by the same within-unit sign-flip procedure applied to the pairwise rank comparisons that constitute the AUC. Because the AUC is a within-unit rank statistic, the exchangeability argument applies identically.

One estimand — the conditional error rate among admitted trials, $\text{CondErr}@\tau = \Pr(y=0 \mid c \ge \tau)$ — is **not a paired quantity**. Its denominator (the admitted set) changes with the condition: at $\tau = 0.90$ the clean arm admits 121 items while the noisy arm admits 61, with an intersection of only 51. The within-item pairing that the permutation test relies on does not exist for this estimand. It is therefore reported as a co-reported quantity and is **not a member of the confirmatory family**.

## 2 The family and direction enforcement

The confirmatory family comprises three hypotheses, tested at family-wise $\alpha = 0.05$ under the Holm sequentially rejective procedure. Let the ordered raw $p$-values be $p_{(1)} \le p_{(2)} \le p_{(3)}$ with corresponding hypotheses $H_{(1)}, H_{(2)}, H_{(3)}$. The Holm thresholds are

$$
\alpha_{(1)} = \frac{0.05}{3} = 0.016\overline{6}, \quad
\alpha_{(2)} = \frac{0.05}{2} = 0.025, \quad
\alpha_{(3)} = 0.05.
\tag{3}
$$

The three hypotheses and their predicted directions are:

- **H1.1**: accuracy falls at $\lambda_{\text{mid}} = 0.18$ (McNemar on paired discordant pairs, with deferred trials counted as errors);
- **H1.2′**: within-arm AUC contrast rises (gate discriminability increases);
- **H1.3**: coverage at $\varepsilon = 0.05$ falls.

Each hypothesis carries its predicted sign as part of its definition. The Holm input is the two-sided $p$-value **only when the observed movement agrees with the predicted direction**. If a contrast is statistically significant but moves in the **opposite** direction, it is recorded as a *direction violation* — a significant finding in its own right — and is **never counted as support** for the hypothesis. This rule is essential: a two-sided $p$-value alone cannot distinguish a predicted effect from a surprise in the wrong direction, and without the rule a genuine refutation would be misreported as confirmation. The original fixed-threshold silent-error hypothesis (H1.2) was exactly such a case: on the development pre-run it moved significantly in the *opposite* direction, and the direction rule converted what a naive two-sided test would call "support" into a documented refutation that motivated the replacement hypothesis H1.2′.
