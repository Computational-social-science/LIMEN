**Current total:** 100,838 items across 6 arms, built 2026-10-10T01:32:54Z (dataset version 2.0.0+n5, 5 options per item, chance 0.2).

## A. Curation rationale

RRC exists to make one question measurable: **whether a language model's errors are visible to
its own confidence depends on the orthographic depth of the script it reads, rather than on the
model.** Testing that requires an error corpus in which the *same* task is posed in scripts of
different depth, and in which the difficulty of the task is controlled by a stated mechanism
rather than by intuition.

The organising frame is the noisy-channel model. A correct form passes through a channel and
emerges corrupted; recovery is decoding. Under that frame, a dataset for this question must
supply, per script, the channel's candidate structure — the set of forms that could plausibly
have been produced — because that set is what makes the depth axis operational. RRC supplies it
as a fixed-choice task: the item shows the corrupted form and asks which candidate is its root.

**RRC is constructed from observed human error only.** Its `provenance` field is `observed` on
every item; the manifest records `provenance_kind: "observed human error only; no simulated
noise"`. Simulated error generators were considered and are recorded as rejected, with reasons,
in `config/datasets.json` — notably LCSTS-IME-2M, which is roughly 50× larger than the Chinese
source used here, uniformly formatted, and constructed by simulating an input method. It was
rejected on the grounds that a study of a channel's real input-error distribution, fed
simulated errors, reports a result about the generator's assumptions rather than about the
channel.

**Position in the empirical chain.** RRC is the parameter-estimation layer of a three-part
chain. (1) The channel's candidate structure and per-script confusability are estimated from
RRC. (2) The provenance discipline above makes each error attributable to a named observed
source, so a degradation can be assigned to a channel rather than to noise in general. (3) The
fixed-choice form gives a per-item ceiling — the item is answerable from the state alone by a
competent reader, and chance is fixed at 1/`n_options` — which is what allows a confidence
calibration result to be read against a bound rather than against zero. The resource supports
testing the claim that input noise is a structural constraint on achievable performance; it
does not, on its own, establish that claim.

| `rrc-en-word` | English | eng | Dsrt, **Latn**, Shaw | shallow |
| `rrc-ru` | Russian | rus | **Cyrl**, Latn | shallow (segmental, non-Latin) |

The writing-system column is the authoritative language-to-script mapping recorded per variety
in LinguaMeta (van Esch, Lucassen, Ruder, Caswell & Rivera, LREC 2022; CC-BY-NC-4.0). The
dominant script for each arm is in bold. Two notes, both consequential:

* **`Jpan` is the standard code for the Japanese mixed script**, so the Japanese arm is not
  "syllabic" — it is a mixed kanji-and-kana system, and the standard already names it.
* **`Hani` is too coarse for the Chinese arms, which are Simplified**: the code is `Hans`.
* The `Latn` present in the Russian and Chinese entries is expected. LinguaMeta records every
  *attested* script for a variety, and measured over the arms, the Russian material is about
  30% Latin by character — identifiers inside code comments in Russian prose. The metadata and
  the measurement agree; the Latin content is a property of the source, not a defect.

The depth labels are labels, not measurements. The quantity they point at is operationalised in
§H.

## C. Speaker demographic

Not applicable in the usual sense, and reported because it is a real property of the source
material rather than an absent field.

The writers are **the populations that produced the source corpora**, and they are not a
controlled or characterised sample. Concretely: the Birkbeck material is amalgamated by its
compiler from native-speaker sections of several error collections and the compiler states it
**includes the efforts of young children and of extremely poor spellers working beyond their
ability**, so its error *rate* is not an adult typist's rate even though its error
*distribution* is usable. The Chinese material is drawn from posts by official media accounts
on a public social platform, so the writers are professional communicators. The GitHub material
is written by software contributors. The Japanese material comes from Wikipedia revision
history, so its writers are Wikipedia editors.

**No claim in this resource is about a population's error rate.** The rates are properties of
the corpora and are reported as such.

## D. Annotator demographic

There are **no annotators**. RRC contains no human judgements and no crowdsourced labels. Every
item is derived mechanically from a source corpus that already recorded a human error and its
correction. Where a source corpus is itself annotated — CSCD-NS is described by its publisher as
40,000 annotated sentences — that annotation is the publisher's, and no annotation was performed
here.

This is a design choice rather than an absence. The dependent variable is the error, and the
error is observed; introducing an annotator would introduce a second, unmeasured judgement
between the phenomenon and its measurement.

## E. Speech situation and text characteristics

**Modality:** written text, entered through a keyboard or an input method.

* `rrc-en`, `rrc-ru`, `rrc-zh-gh` — text committed to a software repository: prose, documentation
  and code comments.
* `rrc-en-word` — single-word British and American misspellings with their intended words.
* `rrc-zh` — short posts on a public social platform.
* `rrc-ja` — article text on a public wiki, captured as revisions.

**Corruption process per arm, as the source actually produced it:**

| Arm | The observed process |
|---|---|
| `rrc-en` | GitHub edits: a corrected form and the form it replaced, in prose files |
| `rrc-en-word` | typists' misspellings of a word, collected from error corpora |
| `rrc-ru` | GitHub edits in Cyrillic lines |
| `rrc-ja` | revision pairs where a character was replaced during kana/kanji input |
| `rrc-zh` | input-method errors: a wrong character committed where the correct one was intended |
| `rrc-zh-gh` | Han-script edits in GitHub prose |

**Item structure.** `state` is the observed corrupted form, `root` is the intended form, and
`candidates` is a five-element list containing the root exactly once. Chosen at build time:
`n_options = 5`, so chance is 0.20. The question form is fixed: *the state is the observed
corrupted form; choose which candidate is its root.* Decoys are the most character-similar
other roots under one formula for all arms (Dice coefficient over character bigrams), and **an
item is kept only if the root is the closest candidate to the state** — so no item is included
in which a decoy is a better fit than the truth by the stated measure.

**All text is stored NFC-normalised.** This is load-bearing rather than cosmetic: the GitHub
corpus contains CJK Compatibility Ideographs (U+FA08 for 行), which are legacy encodings of
characters Unicode also carries in unified form, so without normalisation the same character
appears as two different strings and a compatible-ideograph line looks like it differs from its
own repair.

## F. Collection process

**Nothing was elicited. Every item derives from an existing record of a human error.** The
collection step was acquisition and validation of four published corpora.

| Source | Licence | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Birkbeck Spelling Error Corpus (compiler: Roger Mitton; also Oxford Text Archive handle 20.500.12024/0643) | distributed by the compiler for research use | `rrc-en-word` |
| GitHub Typo Corpus | see the corpus's own terms | `rrc-en`, `rrc-ru`, `rrc-zh-gh` |
| JWTD — Japanese Typo Dataset from Wikipedia revision history | CC-BY-SA 3.0 (the licence of Japanese Wikipedia, as the dataset's own page states) | `rrc-ja` |
| CSCD-NS | MIT | `rrc-zh` |

**Acquisition was verified against the publishers' own figures, not merely downloaded.** For
Birkbeck this is explicit: measurement on disk returns 6,136 correct words and 36,133
misspellings, which is exactly the compiler's published count for that file, and the same
agreement holds for the additional lists. Agreement with the publisher's own number is what
distinguishes the genuine corpus from a partial or substituted download.

**One acquisition was blocked and is recorded because it is not reproducible by trying.**
The Chinese source could not be fetched from this host: `drive.google.com` answered HTTP 000
after a 22-second timeout, and the documented alternative is a Baidu Netdisk link requiring an
account and a client. The file was obtained manually by the programme owner. A later session
finding only the file has no way to know the automated route does not work, which is why the
blocker and its resolution are written down.

## G. Annotation process

Not applicable (§D). The only human judgement in the pipeline is the source corpora's own, and
the only machine judgement is the decoy construction and the validity constraint in §E, both of
which are deterministic given the seed and both of which are recorded in the manifest.

Where a source corpus required interpretation, the interpretation was **measured from the bytes
rather than assumed**, and the measurement is recorded. Two instances:

* **Direction.** In JWTD, `post_loss < pre_loss` holds for 300 of 300 test items, establishing
  that `post_text` is the repair and `pre_text` the human error. The direction was not taken on
  faith from field names.
* **Label semantics.** In CSCD-NS, label 0 marks 21,594 rows whose columns are identical and
  label 1 marks 18,406 rows whose columns differ, establishing the label as an error indicator
  and column 2 as the errored side.
* **Error span.** The Chinese pairs are whole sentences in which one word is wrong. The
  differing span is isolated by trimming the common prefix and suffix, which recovers the
  actual word pair; a position-by-position comparison of the two sentences instead scatters the
  difference and produces spans that are artefacts of the alignment. Extraction succeeds for
  1,951 of the first 2,000 pairs (97.5%).

## H. Quality control

Quality control is a separate artefact, `scripts/rrc_qc.py`, rather than a step inside the
builder, so that it examines the *output* and can therefore catch inconsistencies the builder
cannot see.

**Six structural checks per item:** NFC normalisation of `state` and `root`; candidate
integrity (exactly `n_options` candidates, all distinct, none empty, the root present exactly
once); no decoy equal to the root after normalisation; script composition consistent with the
arm's declared dominant script; provenance present, of kind `observed`, with a named corpus; and
for the reading-based channel layer, that every decoy shares the reading of the position at
which the state differs from the root.

**Plus a published empirical gate.** Amazon Science (Sengupta, Krone & Mansour, NLP4ConvAI 2021)
accept a noisy test set only if "at least 95% of the examples in a sample containing 25% of each
noisy test set are realistic and representative of the given noise type". The gate is
implemented at that sample fraction and threshold, with a mechanical witness function standing
in for the human panel: it does not claim to replace the panel, it claims that an item whose
layer assignment is not witnessed by the item's own fields cannot be realistic, so that class of
failure is caught before a human is asked.

**The checks are proven able to fail.** `--negative-test` corrupts the first item of each arm in
a way each check should catch and asserts the corresponding check goes red. On the current data
it reports 11 findings across three check families and exits non-zero; without that, a passing
run would be an untested check wearing the costume of a passing one.

**Current status:** 2,802 items sampled across the 6 arms, 19,973 checks run, all pass. The suite now carries a **seventh** family, `validity`, which tests the manifest's stated validity constraint on the layers where it is defined: on the ranked layers (L2, L3del, L3) every retained item must have a strictly positive margin, and on the unranked layers (L1, the observed-variant layer, and L4, the shuffled unrelated-form control) no margin may be present at all, because the root is not expected to be nearest there. That family was added after the constraint was found to be documented in the manifest and never applied in the builder, and it found a further inconsistency in the builder on its first run.

**The depth axis is operationalised, not asserted.** For each script the confusable layer is
drawn from that script's own channel:

| Script class | Channel | Source |
|---|---|---|
| alphabetic, abjad, abugida | the neighbouring key on that language's layout | published: MuLTYPO (Zhao et al., ACL 2026) over kbdlayout.info |
| Han (`Hans`) | the input-method candidate set — characters sharing the reading of the errored position | **not published**; measured here with `pypinyin` |
| Japanese (`Jpan`) | the kana flick group | **not published**; measured here from CLDR flick keyboard data |

The two non-alphabetic channels are the part of this design that no published work covers. This
was verified by running the published multilingual generator rather than by reading its paper:
`multypo` v0.1.1 generates typos for `english` and `russian` and raises `Unsupported language`
for `chinese` and `japanese`, consistent with its twelve supported scripts being alphabetic,
abjad or abugida. The keyboard model assumes one key to one character and calls an error a
neighbouring keypress; an input method maps one key to many through a phonetic intermediate and
its error arises in candidate selection. Forcing either into the keyboard abstraction would
produce items that look like keystroke errors while measuring something else, so they are built
separately.

**An empirical anchor for the keyboard channel.** The 136M-Keystrokes corpus (Dhakal, Feit,
Kristensson & Oulasvirta, CHI 2018) measures real typing: 168,593 participant files, and a
published uncorrected error rate of 1.167% per keystroke (SD 1.43%). It also supplies a warning
about measurement: diffing the target sentence against the produced text reports 91% of
participants carrying an uncorrected error, because a sentence left unfinished and a key pressed
without shift both count as disagreements. The two figures differ by roughly 78×, entirely
definitionally.

**Difficulty is recorded per layer, and for the IME layer it is not a margin.** For the ranked layers the item records `margin`, the root's similarity to the state minus the best decoy's, and the builder requires it to be positive. For the IME layer (`L2c`) that quantity does not exist: its candidates are homophones *by construction* -- sharing the reading of the errored position is the mechanism the layer models -- so the character-bigram margin is 0 for every pair of distinct single characters while a reading-based margin would be 1 for every pair. The field that does characterise a frequency-ordered channel is `group_size`, the number of characters the channel offers for that reading; its median over the Chinese arm is 14. The fixed-choice form shows five options, so it **understates** the difficulty of that channel.

**The validity constraint applies to the ranked layers only**, and this scoping is part of the manifest text rather than an implementation detail. Applying it to every layer without scoping was tried and cut the Chinese arm from 12,351 items to 294, because that arm is almost entirely L2c, L4 and L1 -- none of which is ranked.

## I. Aligned passages

None. RRC is not a parallel corpus and contains no translation pairs. Each item is a single
corrupted form with its candidate set.

Items in different arms are **not aligned to one another**, and this is a property a user must know. The arms carry 2,337 (`rrc-en`), 84,001 (`rrc-en-word`), 302 (`rrc-ru`), 1,076 (`rrc-ja`), 11,590 (`rrc-zh`), 1,532 (`rrc-zh-gh`). They differ in item count, in which candidate layers they support, and in object size (§K).

## J. Uses

**Intended use.** Evaluation of a model's ability to recover an intended form from an observed
corruption when the candidate set is supplied; and measurement of whether the model's confidence
separates its correct answers from its errors, per script.

**A secondary use the corpus was designed for** is as a *validity criterion* for a generated
manipulation. Where a synthetic typo generator claims a per-script error distribution and an
observed corpus exists for the same language, the observed one tests the claim. That role is
stronger than being one point on a curve.

**Not intended, and stated explicitly:**

* **Not a benchmark for spelling correction in deployment.** The candidate sets are closed and
  constructed; a system that scores well here has not been shown to correct unrestricted text.
* **Not a source of population error rates.** The writers are not a characterised or
  representative sample (§C); rates belong to the corpora.
* **Not evidence of parity across scripts.** The arms are not aligned (§I) and do not carry the
  same layers; cross-arm comparison requires the confound in §K to be handled explicitly.
* **Not a training set for a commercial model where a source licence forbids it.** See §L.

## K. Other

**Known limitations, carried verbatim from the manifest** because they are the resource's own
statement of what it cannot support:

1. Confusability is character-level; a proxy for the linguistic confusability the depth axis
   describes, not a measurement of it.
2. Birkbeck states are single tokens while JWTD and CSCD states are sentence-length, so the arms
   differ in object size even with a shared decoy rule.
3. Birkbeck is a pre-LLM-era spelling corpus; its errors are not keyboard-shaped.

## L. Disclosures

**Funding and provenance of the labour.** Not applicable; the resource was constructed from
published corpora by the programme that publishes it. No annotation was crowdsourced and no
participant was paid.

**Speaker and annotator compensation.** Not applicable (§C, §D): no speakers and no annotators.

**Licence of RRC itself.** RRC is a derived work and cannot be licensed more permissively than
its sources on every arm. The sources carry different terms: CSCD-NS is MIT; JWTD is CC-BY-SA
3.0, which is a copyleft term that travels with derived material; and the Birkbeck corpus is
distributed by its compiler for research use, referenced to an Oxford Text Archive handle. **A
user intending to redistribute an arm must check that arm's source term**, and the per-arm
provenance in `config/rrc.json` names the source for exactly this purpose.

**Third-party resources referenced rather than redistributed.** The writing-system metadata
(LinguaMeta) is CC-BY-NC-4.0; the Amazon Science noise benchmarks are CC-BY-NC-4.0; the keyboard
data is Unicode CLDR. These inform the design and are cited; they are not part of the
distributed corpus.

**Intended use that must be declared by a user.** The corpora are built from public user
generated content — social platform posts, wiki edits, public software repositories. No personal
identifiers are introduced by RRC, but a downstream user re-joining arms to their sources
inherits the sources' own terms and should treat the material as public-but-attributable rather
than anonymous.

**Known risk of misuse.** The reading-based layers encode a language's homophone structure
(`pypinyin` for Han). Used outside this study, such a structure could be applied to de-anonymise
or to reconstruct intended text from corrupted text. The resource is published for robustness
measurement and confidence-calibration research.

## How to reproduce

```
python scripts/build_rrc.py --options 5      # writes data/rrc/*.jsonl and config/rrc.json
python scripts/rrc_qc.py                     # structural checks + the published empirical gate
python scripts/rrc_qc.py --negative-test     # proves the checks can fail
```

Input corpora are located through `RRC_CORPORA_DIR`, an environment variable with no hard-coded
default; the build fails loudly if it is unset or if a required corpus is missing. Seeds and all
counts are recorded in `config/rrc.json`.
